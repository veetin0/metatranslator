# Research: sign language recognition tooling (as of 2026-11-13)

Method: ~8 web searches. Items marked **[unverified]** are from my background knowledge or aggregator pages and were not confirmed by a primary source. Items marked **[not found]** had no usable source.

## 1. Pose/landmark pipelines and pretrained models
- **MediaPipe** (Hand/Pose/Face Landmarker, Holistic legacy) runs on web (`@mediapipe/tasks-vision`, WASM/WebGL, real time on laptop/phone), Android, iOS and Python. Doing the landmarks in-browser keeps video off the network, which helps R3 (consent). [unverified: exact fps on phone; plan on 15–30 fps on a laptop]
- **GISLR (Google Isolated Sign Language Recognition, Kaggle, Feb–May 2023, $100k)**: 250 ASL signs, MediaPipe Holistic landmarks as input. The rules required a TFLite model under 40 MB with inference under 100 ms per sample. Winners: 1st = 1D-CNN + Transformer; 2nd = EfficientNet-B0 on 160x80 landmark "images" plus DeBERTa/BERT; 3rd = ensemble of 6 conv1d + 2 Transformers. Public repos exist (e.g. github.com/ffs333/2nd_place_GISLR, TheoViel/kaggle_islr). The top models are fast (CPU, <100 ms) but are ensembles; single-model variants are lighter. [unverified: the ~0.89 leaderboard accuracy is from memory]
  - Sources: https://github.com/ffs333/2nd_place_GISLR , https://relatedrepos.com/gh/TheoViel/kaggle_islr
  - Caveat: trained on landmarks from ASL-signers filmed on selfie phone cameras, with Holistic landmarks. The landmark format must match exactly if we reuse weights (the Holistic indices; the Tasks API outputs differ slightly). Reusing the weights in a browser means a TFLite→TF.js/ONNX conversion, which is risky for 36 h.
- **PopSign ASL v1.0** (NeurIPS 2023): 250 signs, >210,000 examples, 47 Deaf signers, Pixel 4a selfie camera. Baseline LSTM on MediaPipe landmarks: 84.2% test (82.1% val). PopSignAI (IUI 2026) reports 82.9% user-independent LSTM with a 2.5 MB model and ~7 ms inference. Best fit for "phone-class real time". https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/00dada608b8db212ea7d9d92b24c68de-Abstract.html
- **ASL Fingerspelling Kaggle (2023)**: phrase-level fingerspelling, TFLite winners; [not verified in this pass; known to be large Transformer models, hand-only landmarks, good for letters but needs a clear hand view].
- **WLASL** (2000 glosses, internet videos): top-1 on WLASL2000 ≈ 32% (original I3D) up to ~63% (Uni-Sign, RGB+pose, per aggregator pages; unverified), pose-only ≈ 47% (SLGTformer). Noisy labels; not usable as-is for a demo. MS-ASL: similar, partly dead links [unverified]. How2Sign: continuous ASL, for translation research, no off-the-shelf real-time model. ASL Citizen (~84k videos, 2.7k signs, Sem-Lex benchmark): the best modern isolated dataset, with I3D/ST-GCN baselines available. https://arxiv.org/html/2310.00196v1
- **2025–26 open models**: Uni-Sign (ICLR 2025, code on GitHub) is an RGB+pose LLM-style model for ISLR/CSLR/SLT: GPU-class, not real time on a phone; usable on a Modal GPU as an offline/batch demo. SignLLM is sign *production* (text→sign), irrelevant here. sign.mt (EMNLP 2024 demo) is real-time multilingual sign↔text, open, but CC BY-NC-SA, and its sign→text quality is research-grade [unverified]. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.15187v2 , https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-demo.19
- **Real time on phone/laptop**: landmark + small LSTM/1D-CNN/Transformer (PopSign, GISLR-style). **Needs GPU (Modal)**: Uni-Sign and any RGB/video backbone (I3D, VideoMAE, Swin).
- **No open model does continuous sentence-level ASL in real time at usable accuracy.** Say so in the pitch (BRIEF A2).

## 2. Vision-language model approach
- Evidence is poor. FLEURS-ASL (NAACL 2025): GPT-4o, Claude 3 Opus and Gemini 1.5 Pro show "virtually no understanding" of ASL. https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13585v1
- A 2026 zero-shot isolated-sign benchmark on WLASL300 (arXiv 2604.xxxxx; page listed at https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2604.11225 — treat as unconfirmed) finds most VLMs near random; Gemini-2.5-Pro is best at ~32% with all 300 glosses listed in the prompt (a closed-set prompt helps). An AlphaSignal summary says Gemini 2.5 Pro tops a sign-language benchmark but trails experts by ~65 points. https://alphasignal.ai/news/gemini-2-5-pro-tops-sign-language-benchmark-but-still-trails-experts-by-65
- Takeaway: a VLM can work as a **closed-set tie-breaker/verifier** (give it a candidate list of ~20–40 glosses plus the clip), not as a free-form ASL translator. Gemini 3 results [not found; test it ourselves in 15 min on the clips we record].
- Latency/cost: a 2–4 s clip through the Gemini API takes ~1–3 s round trip; Live API streams video at ~1 fps, which is too coarse for signs [unverified]. Cost is small at demo scale. Credits are available (BRIEF).

## 3. Hybrid: few-shot custom vocabulary + LLM sentence smoothing
- Pipeline: MediaPipe landmarks → normalise (wrist-relative, scale by shoulder width; hand joint angles are more rotation/scale-invariant) → kNN/DTW or a tiny classifier → gloss stream with confidence → LLM (Claude/Gemini) turns glosses + context into a fluent English sentence.
- Evidence on example counts: **no source gives a curve** [not found]. Related: naive normalised DTW+kNN reached only ~19% on 2.7k-class ASL Citizen (arXiv 2510.07453), which is a large-vocabulary, user-independent setting, so it does not transfer to 20–40 classes. A 2026 few-shot hand-keypoint paper says prototypes from K examples are very sensitive to variance, and that angle features beat raw coordinates by up to 25 points (arXiv 2603.09213). A LIBRAS study shows that a *subset* of landmarks (hands + a few upper-body/pose points) is more accurate than all 543 and 5x faster (arXiv 2510.24887).
- Our estimate (**unverified, to be measured**): 15–30 recordings per sign, from 2–3 signers, with augmentation (mirror, small rotations/time-warp), gives ~90–95% on a 20–30 sign closed vocabulary for the same signers in the same setting; expect 70–85% for a new signer. Choose visually distinct signs (avoid minimal pairs). Use a confidence threshold plus "did you mean…" rather than forcing an answer.
- Cheap upgrade: train a small 1D-CNN/GRU on the landmark sequences (seconds on CPU) once we have ≥15 clips per sign; keep kNN/DTW as a baseline.
- Fingerspelling: static-hand-letter classifier on MediaPipe hand keypoints works well for ~20 static letters (J, Z move) [unverified]; letters are slow in conversation, so treat as a name-spelling feature.

## 4. Non-manual markers
- MediaPipe Face Landmarker outputs 52 blendshapes (browJoin/browInnerUp for raised/furrowed brows, mouth shapes, head pose from the facial transformation matrix) at real-time speed in web/mobile. Quick wins: raised eyebrows + head tilt → yes/no question flag; head shake → negation; furrowed brow → wh-question. Heuristic thresholds only, ~1–2 h to wire, no trained model, and accuracy is anecdotal [no benchmark found]. Show it as a tag ("question?"), not as a translation claim. For the LLM, pass the flag so it chooses "?" punctuation.

## 5. Finnish Sign Language (FinSL)
- **Corpus of Finnish Sign Language (CFinSL)**, University of Jyväskylä, on the Language Bank of Finland (Kielipankki): ~15 h of video from 21 signers (narratives + conversation), ELAN annotations, linked to the Finnish Signbank lexical database; the compendium lists a larger FinSL/FinSSL collection (104 signers, 15.25 h open). Licence records conflict (CC BY-NC 4.0 vs "In Copyright"): check before use. Too small to train a recognizer, but useful for the pilot story. https://urn.fi/urn:nbn:fi:lb-2019012321 , https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lr/compendium/corpus/cfinsl.html , https://www.sign-lang.uni-hamburg.de/lrec/pub/20004.html
- **Suvi** (suvi.viittomat.net), the online FinSL dictionary by Kuurojen Liitto (2003): a dictionary and video reference for choosing our demo vocabulary; no data licence found. Kuurojen Liitto (Finnish Association of the Deaf) is the natural pilot partner [contact details not researched].
- No public FinSL isolated-sign ML dataset found [not found]. Plan: demo with ASL (data/models exist), pitch FinSL via recording a local vocabulary with a Finnish signer. Because the few-shot pipeline is language-agnostic, switching to FinSL is just re-recording the vocabulary, which is a strong claim for the Helsinki pilot.

## 6. Camera viewpoint (glasses camera)
- **DAT stream limits** (Meta Wearables Device Access Toolkit): max 720x1280 (portrait), presets 720x1280 / 504x896 / 360x640, frame rates 2/7/15/24/30 fps; over Bluetooth, the SDK lowers resolution first, then fps; Meta states lower presets can look *better* due to less compression. https://mixed-news.com/en/meta-dat-camera-720x1280-lower-resolution-higher-quality/ , https://github.com/facebook/meta-wearables-dat-ios . Not found: whether a web app can reach the camera (BRIEF Q2); the DAT iOS/Android route is documented.
- Issues: (a) portrait 720x1280 is OK for a single signer centred at 1–2 m; hands are ~60–100 px, borderline for finger-level detail, so fingerspelling will suffer at >1.5 m; (b) lossy Bluetooth compression + motion blur at ≤15 fps degrades fast hand motion; (c) wearer head movement adds global motion: normalise relative to the signer's shoulders/torso, not the image frame; (d) signers sign in front of the chest/face and can leave the frame, so tell the wearer to face them and stay at ~1–1.5 m; (e) ordinary signing (Ray-Ban camera ~12 MP wide POV) is fine laterally; (f) latency of Bluetooth + phone relay ~0.3–1 s [unverified].
- Mitigations: run MediaPipe on the phone/laptop receiving the stream, not on the glasses; use pose-relative normalisation; crop around the signer using pose then re-run hand landmarks on the crop to gain effective resolution; ask for a "sign slower, in front of chest" calibration step; show a framing hint ("move closer / hands not visible") on the glasses display; test the Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 vs Display camera early. Also build the **laptop-webcam fallback** (BRIEF A1) with the same pipeline.

## 7. Recommendation
**Stack (36 h)**: Glasses camera frames (DAT iOS/Android app, or webcam fallback) → MediaPipe Hand + Pose (+ Face for non-manual flags) in the client → normalised landmark windows → **closed-vocabulary classifier over ~30 self-recorded classroom/everyday signs** (kNN/DTW first, small GRU/1D-CNN if time) with confidence + segmentation by "hands rise/fall" → gloss queue → **Claude/Gemini** call to turn glosses (+ question flag) into one fluent sentence → text on the Display + TTS. Optional: Gemini clip verifier restricted to the candidate list when confidence is low.
- Hear→sign reply path (I1): speech-to-text of the hearing wearer shown to the signer on a phone/tablet [out of scope here].
- **Expected accuracy**: 85–95% top-1 on the 30-sign vocabulary for the team's own signers in controlled lighting; ~70–85% for unseen signers; fingerspelling lower. Not open-vocabulary translation. Say this on stage (I5).
- **Time-boxing**: day 1 record 25 clips x 30 signs (≈1 h with 2–3 people) and get kNN working; day 2 add the LLM smoothing, TTS, glasses UI, and non-manual flag.
- **Fallback A**: PopSign/GISLR-style pretrained 250-sign LSTM, if someone can convert/port it quickly (landmark-format mismatch risk). **Fallback B**: Gemini 2.5/3 clip classification limited to a 30-gloss list (expect ≈30–60% [unverified], so only as a verifier). **Fallback C** (demo safety): "Wizard of Oz" button to inject the signed phrase, labelled honestly, so the glasses UX still demos.
- Use Modal ($100/person) only for training a small model on recorded data or for a Uni-Sign offline showcase; not needed for the live loop.
