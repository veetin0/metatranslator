The problem

Everyday conversations have no live translation between ASL and English.

  • Interpreters are booked ahead for big moments, not for asking where the book is or saying thank you.
  • Typing on a phone breaks eye contact and turns a talk into passing notes.
  • The burden of being understood falls on the Deaf person, every time.

Who it is for

A hearing teacher, classmate or desk worker wearing the glasses, and the Deaf signer in front of them, in short everyday exchanges.

What we are not

Everyday phrases only. Not an interpreter.

  • A small vocabulary of everyday ASL signs, not full ASL grammar.
  • For medical, legal and exams, book an interpreter.
  • When unsure, it says so, or shows "Didn't catch that".

Consent and data

  • The signer starts and stops it, from their own card.
  • Frames live in memory only; never written, never uploaded.
  • No face ID, no signer profile. Taught signs are deleted in one tap.

How it ships

A real glasses app, built on Meta's Wearables Device Access Toolkit.

  • iOS app on DAT: one session holds the glasses camera and the display.
  • Caption cards on the 600×600 lens; speech through the glasses speaker.
  • Phone to laptop over USB; captions never leave the local network.

Fallback

The same lens as a Display Web App, testable in Meta's Display Simulator without the glasses.
